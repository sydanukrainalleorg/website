A demining dog teaches children in Ukraine how to stay safe

In frontline cities such as Kharkiv, Chivas, a trained mine detection dog, helps Save the Children teach children to recognise explosive hazards.

Save the Children has told the story of Chivas, a six-year-old German Spitz trained as a mine detection dog. Besides detection work, he takes part in mine safety lessons for children in frontline cities such as Kharkiv, where he signals danger by crossing his paws.

Save the Children has run explosive ordnance risk education in Ukraine together with the Ukrainian Deminers Association since 2022. According to the organisation, one in ten landmine casualties in Ukraine is a child, and about 23% of the country's land is potentially contaminated with mines and unexploded ordnance.

The aim of the lessons is simple: every child should know how to recognise danger and what to do when they see it.

Our reminder: if you see a suspicious object, do not approach or touch it, move away the same way you came and call the emergency number (101 in Ukraine, 112 in the EU). Risk education is one of the five pillars of mine action, and dogs make it memorable for children.
