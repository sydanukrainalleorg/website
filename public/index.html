Safe land. Safe return.

We train mine detection dogs and their instructors, and develop modern systems for detecting and neutralising explosive ordnance, from drones to bee-based detection, so that Ukraine’s land can be made safe faster and with fewer losses.

See how it works Partner with us

Today we train mine detection dogs, their handlers and specialists, and develop detection technology. Clearance is carried out by accredited operators. We are preparing to apply for our own demining licence in Ukraine.

Our mission

To help make Ukraine’s land safe again, by developing better ways to find explosive ordnance and by helping people stay safe until their land is cleared.

We see a Ukraine where every family can return home and every farmer can work their fields without fear.

Since 2022, landmines and unexploded ordnance have spread across farmland, forests, villages and infrastructure in Ukraine. Clearing them with traditional methods alone will take decades.

Detection technology

No single method finds every hazard. We combine biological detection, aerial data and machines, so that each one covers the weaknesses of the others.

Core programme · training active

Detection dogs and their handlers

We train mine detection dogs and the instructors who work with them. A dog and handler team can check suspected ground quickly and precisely.

Training follows international standards for animal detection, with regular testing of every team. It takes place at our training ground near Iisalmi, North Savo, Finland.

Join the dog programme

Role in the system

Precise check of the zones that bees and drones have flagged, before clearance begins.

Innovation · pilot project

Bees that lead us to explosives

Honey bees have an exceptional sense of smell. During training, we place sugar feeders next to the scent of explosives, so bees learn to link that scent with food.

When released over an area, trained bees gather where explosive scent is present. Drones record where they cluster, and the clusters show us where to look first.

Role in the system

Fast first look at large areas. Bees cover ground that would take people weeks, and they are too light to set off a mine.

In development

Robotics and mechanised tools

We develop remote-controlled and mechanised equipment, both in-house and with partners. Machines prepare the ground, clear vegetation and take on the most dangerous work.

Drones with cameras and sensors connect every part of the system into one map.

Role in the system

Keep people at a distance from danger and speed up work on large areas.

Research

African giant pouched rats

These rats have a strong sense of smell and are too light to set off a mine. The method is already proven in several countries.

We are studying how it can work in Ukraine’s climate and conditions, and which partners could bring it here.

Role in the system

A possible addition to dog teams for detailed checks of suspected ground.

What we do

Our work covers the five pillars of humanitarian mine action defined by the International Mine Action Standards.

  • Training

    We train detection dogs, their instructors and specialists who will work in the field.

  • Detection technology and survey

    We develop tools and methods that show faster where explosive hazards are, and help partners plan their work.

  • Risk education

    We teach children and adults how to recognise danger and act safely, with digital and AI-assisted materials.

  • Victim assistance

    We help survivors and their families find medical, rehabilitation and psychological support through trusted partners.

  • Advocacy

    We raise awareness in Finland, Europe, the UK and the US of the long-term cost of explosive contamination.

Offer your help

Join our dog programme

We train mine detection dogs and their handlers at our own training ground. To grow the programme, we are looking for dogs and people.

Kennels and breeders

We are not a breeding kennel. We are looking for kennels and owners ready to donate puppies or young dogs with a strong nose and a drive to search: for example Belgian and Dutch Shepherds, German Shepherds, Labrador and Golden Retrievers, English Springer and Cocker Spaniels, beagles and Jack Russell terriers. Mixed breeds are welcome too, because character matters more than pedigree. We assess each dog together with the breeder or owner and keep you informed about its training.

Future dog handlers

Would you like to become a dog handler? Previous experience is not required: we will train you. We are looking for motivated volunteers who are ready to work with a dog regularly at our training ground.

Our training ground is near Iisalmi, North Savo, Finland.
